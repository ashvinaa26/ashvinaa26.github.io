Who matters in a network?

107 characters from A Storm of Swords, linked when their names appear within 15 words of each other. Three measures answer three different questions: who has the most connections, who bridges different groups, and who is linked to the characters who matter most.

  • DataNetwork of Thrones (Beveridge & Shan, 2016)
  • Coverage107 characters, 352 links
  • ToolsPython, pandas, NetworkX, Plotly
  • ContextMSIN0167 class exercise, UCL

Open the chart full screen

What to notice

The most connected character is not the top broker. Tyrion has the most links (36 of a possible 106), but Jon has the highest brokerage score (0.230): many of the shortest routes between other characters pass through him, because he connects groups that are otherwise far apart. On eigenvector centrality, which rewards links to well-connected characters, Jon is only 15th.

How to use it

  • The three measure buttons change the dot sizes and colours, the top-8 bars and the title.
  • Hover a dot to see a character’s links and all three scores with their ranks.
  • Open “All values as a table” under the chart to read every value without hovering.
New in my version: the Highlight a character menu (top right of the chart) draws one character’s links in orange, rings everyone they are linked to and names their strongest link. It shows who a character connects, not just how central they are: Jon, for example, links the Night’s Watch, the wildlings and the Starks.

A link means two names appear close together in the text, not that the characters met or are friends; enemies are linked too, and some characters (Robert, for example) are linked through other people’s memories. First names only: “Jon” is Jon Snow.
