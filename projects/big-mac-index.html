The Big Mac index: which currencies look over- or undervalued?

A Big Mac is almost the same everywhere, so its price should buy about the same in every country. Comparing the prices shows which currencies look too strong or too weak, at market exchange rates and after allowing for income.

  • DataThe Economist, Big Mac index
  • Coverage45 readings, 2000–2026
  • ToolsPython, pandas, Plotly, matplotlib
  • ContextMSIN0167 class exercise, UCL

Open the chart full screenStatic picture (PNG)

What to notice

In July 2026 a Big Mac in Switzerland costs 45% more than in America, and in Indonesia 62% less. Adjusting for income (GDP per person) barely changes the typical gap: 27% raw, 25% adjusted.

How to use it

  • Compare with switches the base currency (US dollar, euro, British pound, Japanese yen, Chinese yuan).
  • Show switches between raw, GDP-adjusted, or both.
  • Play or the slider moves through 45 readings from 2000 to 2026; the rows re-rank as you go.
  • Hover a row for its prices and rank; click a row to pin its history.
New in my version: once a country is pinned, add a second one (the box marked NEW in the panel, or Shift+click a row). Both histories are drawn together, so you can see how two currencies have moved against each other over 26 years, which the original only shows one at a time.
